# Project Vision

## 1. Purpose

AI Revenue Lab exists to test whether abundant AI production can create direct revenue, not merely reduce labor cost.

The central hypothesis is:

> Free, high-volume, and replaceable AI models can produce information, stories, and personalized digital products at a quantity, speed, and degree of individual adaptation that would be economically impossible for human teams.

This repository is not organized around promoting a particular model. It is organized around testing a new production system.

## 2. How the idea evolved

The project began with the observation that AI can make previously uneconomic work feasible.

The hypothesis then developed through four stages:

1. **Automation** — AI performs translation, classification, writing, and verification work.
2. **Mass production** — AI handles countries, languages, topics, and content volumes that a human organization cannot cover economically.
3. **Real-time adaptation** — user comments, selections, and current context can influence the next output within minutes or hours.
4. **Mass personalization** — one common source can become millions of different editions, each reflecting one person's interests, situation, memory, and prior reactions.

The final implication is larger than content automation:

> Industrialization supplied the same product cheaply to everyone. AI can supply a different product cheaply to everyone.

## 3. Product principle

The unit of value is not only the source object. It is the relationship between the source and the individual.

A convenience store in Bangnim-dong is physically one place, but its meaning differs by person and moment. For one person it is where cigarettes were purchased; for another it is where a cola was purchased; for another it is a former workplace or a memory from a rainy night.

AI-native products should therefore combine:

- shared facts;
- shared source material;
- individual meaning;
- current context;
- delivery timing;
- continuing user feedback.

The governing rule is:

> Facts remain shared. Meaning and presentation may be personalized.

## 4. What must be demonstrated

The project is successful only when it produces measurable evidence that AI-generated products create economic value.

Every product experiment should test some combination of the following claims:

- AI can cover information that human media do not economically cover.
- AI can react while the user's interest is still active.
- AI can produce an individual edition for a market of one.
- AI-created outputs can earn advertising, affiliate, subscription, purchase, licensing, or service revenue.
- The cash production cost can remain substantially below the economic value produced.

## 5. Initial product families

### Personalized world feed

Global-local information on places, festivals, entertainment, culture, and sports is collected, translated, verified, and edited into a different world edition for each user.

### Living travel

A reader receives a continuing travel letter. Their reaction to today's edition changes tomorrow's edition. The series may eventually become a personal travel book.

### Living fiction

A common fictional world supports a shared canonical story and optional reader-specific branches, viewpoints, side stories, and endings. Reader choices and comments can affect subsequent outputs quickly.

### Personal edition

Conversations, decisions, memories, and daily records are transformed into polished letters, magazines, reports, and books rather than being left as transient chat logs.

## 6. Strategic boundary

This project should not become a general-purpose AI chat interface. Chat is an input method and production conversation. The delivered product should be a finished experience:

- edited;
- structured;
- visually presentable;
- scheduled or delivered at the right moment;
- persistent;
- responsive to the next user reaction.

The conversation is the production process. The user receives the work.

## 7. Long-term platform thesis

The shared platform may eventually become a Personal Edition Engine:

```text
common sources and conversations
→ individual meaning and context
→ research and generation
→ editorial packaging
→ timed delivery
→ user response
→ next personal edition
```

Its long-term purpose is to provide each person with a small private editorial office capable of understanding, producing, and continuously revising that person's edition of the world.
