# Initial Product Portfolio

## 1. Portfolio purpose

The repository should test the same AI production thesis through different revenue mechanisms rather than assuming one product category will succeed.

The initial portfolio contains three primary experiments and several later candidates.

## 2. Experiment A — Personalized World Feed

### User value

A user receives a personal edition of global-local information covering places, festivals, culture, entertainment, music, film, and selected sports information that large global media rarely surface.

### AI advantage being tested

- simultaneous coverage across countries and languages;
- translation and structuring at high volume;
- rapid detection of updates;
- personal interpretation of shared facts;
- repeated reuse of one source across many individual editions.

### Initial low-risk scope

- local events and festivals;
- tourism, nature, and cultural experiences;
- official entertainment releases and schedules;
- official sports schedules and results;
- newly announced public programs and places.

### Initial exclusions

- politics and elections;
- crime allegations;
- war, disaster, and death reporting;
- medical and financial advice;
- celebrity private life;
- anonymous rumors;
- content concerning minors without a clear public-interest and lawful basis.

### Possible revenue

- advertising;
- accommodation, ticket, tour, and commerce affiliates;
- premium personal editions;
- paid alerts;
- promoted regional listings;
- B2B data or white-label feeds.

## 3. Experiment B — Living Fiction

### User value

Readers share a canonical fictional world while receiving optional branches, viewpoints, side stories, and endings that reflect their selections and feedback.

### AI advantage being tested

- rapid reaction to comments and voting;
- large-scale testing of premises and openings;
- continuation of many narrative paths;
- individual editions without individual human authors;
- conversion of successful text IP into audio, illustration, or comics.

### Product structure

- shared world rules and canonical storyline;
- reader-specific optional branches;
- transparent distinction between official canon and personal edition;
- feedback analysis that corrects continuity errors but does not blindly surrender long-term narrative quality to majority preference.

### Possible revenue

- episode subscriptions;
- branch or character passes;
- alternative endings;
- personal completed editions;
- ebooks and audiobooks;
- adaptation and IP licensing.

## 4. Experiment C — Living Travel / Personal Edition

### User value

A reader receives a continuing travel letter or personal magazine. Their reaction to today's edition changes the next edition, which arrives as a polished product rather than a raw chatbot answer.

### AI advantage being tested

- market-of-one production;
- delayed editorial delivery despite near-instant production;
- continuous learning from explicit feedback;
- transformation of transient conversation into a persistent work;
- eventual compilation into a personal book.

### Example loop

```text
Day 1: general destination edition
→ user asks for fewer famous attractions and more local food
Day 2: neighborhood restaurants and markets
→ user asks for quiet places suitable for solo travel
Day 3: quiet solo-friendly route and morning dining
```

### Possible revenue

- daily or weekly subscription;
- destination season pass;
- travel-time premium edition;
- affiliate bookings;
- final personal travel book;
- printed annual or memory editions.

## 5. Later candidates

- personal life magazine from conversations and records;
- family newspaper;
- personal meaning map;
- children's adaptive stories;
- personal fan magazine;
- individualized sports reports;
- interest-based language learning magazine;
- founder strategy letter;
- personal audio channel;
- media creation platform that lets creators operate their own AI editorial office;
- personalized local-commerce newsletters.

## 6. Selection criteria

A product is a strong candidate when it meets most of the following:

- more production creates more value;
- real-time response changes the product materially;
- individual production would be uneconomic for humans;
- outputs remain reusable or accumulative;
- quality can be measured automatically or through user behavior;
- a plausible payment, affiliate, advertising, or licensing path exists;
- the experience is more than an unformatted AI chat response.

## 7. Portfolio rule

The experiments should share infrastructure only where it accelerates learning. The repository must avoid building a large general platform before one user-facing loop demonstrates engagement or revenue.

The first implementation should therefore test one thin end-to-end loop, while the other product families remain documented rather than simultaneously built.
