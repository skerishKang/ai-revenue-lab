# Fan Magazine Reference Notes

- Research date: **2026-07-26 (Asia/Seoul)**
- Research boundary: publicly accessible official product, publication, and support pages.
- Usage boundary: references informed hierarchy, rhythm, archive treatment, and motion only. No third-party screen, brand asset, copy block, photograph, or illustration is embedded.

## Adjacent products

### 1. Weverse Magazine

- URL: `https://magazine.weverse.io/`
- Observed pattern: Korean-first editorial categories, prominent subject imagery, interviews, reviews, playlists, and dated cultural coverage.
- Adopted: clear article taxonomy, confident Korean display headlines, editorial date and category labels.
- Rejected: real artist ecosystem, promotional adjacency, current-entertainment-news breadth, and any community/platform identity.
- Fan Magazine difference: one fictional subject is edited for one reader as a finite issue, not published as a broad artist-news portal.

### 2. MUBI Notebook

- URL: `https://mubi.com/en/notebook`
- Observed pattern: cinema publication framing, large still-led features, criticism, interviews, and restrained metadata.
- Adopted: image and criticism treated as one spread, quiet source lines, substantial opening paragraphs.
- Rejected: streaming-service adjacency, film-catalog navigation, and a general cinema readership.
- Fan Magazine difference: the issue follows one fictional creator across current work, archive rediscovery, and a private editor note.

### 3. Criterion Current

- URL: `https://www.criterion.com/current`
- Supporting design note: `https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/5617-welcome-to-our-new-site`
- Observed pattern: essays and archive materials presented with magazine layouts and strong film-history context.
- Adopted: archival chronology, essay-like captions, historical material reframed through present reading.
- Rejected: commerce/catalog integration, canonical film collection authority, and product purchase pathways.
- Fan Magazine difference: the archive is synthetic, personal, non-commercial, and organized around one reader's recurring attention.

### 4. Apple News personalization

- URL: `https://support.apple.com/ko-kr/guide/iphone/iph5b557ed3d/26/ios/26`
- Observed pattern: editorial selection combines followed channels/topics with curated stories.
- Adopted: the conceptual premise that editorial choice can reflect an individual preference.
- Rejected: infinite feed, topic-follow controls, news cards, breaking-news hierarchy, and live recommendation behavior.
- Fan Magazine difference: personalization is expressed as a completed issue artifact, not exposed as feed mechanics or algorithm settings.

### 5. Letterboxd

- URL: `https://letterboxd.com/about/`
- Supporting diary/import reference: `https://letterboxd.com/about/importing-data/`
- Observed pattern: a person's taste becomes legible through diary entries, lists, favorites, and reviews.
- Adopted: personal memory can sit beside cultural works without becoming a generic profile.
- Rejected: social activity, ratings, comments, public identity, popularity lists, and statistics.
- Fan Magazine difference: the reader appears only through a restrained editor note embedded in the issue.

## Editorial and design references

### 6. The Gentlewoman

- URL: `https://thegentlewoman.co.uk/magazine/n-26`
- Adopted: dominant portrait treatment, sharp masthead hierarchy, restrained palette, issue identity, intimate long-form interview tone.
- Rejected: recognizable real-person cover language and direct imitation of its typography or grid.

### 7. Kinfolk Stories

- URL: `https://www.kinfolk.com/stories/`
- Adopted: cream paper atmosphere, measured whitespace, quiet cultural authority, and object-focused editorial imagery.
- Rejected: lifestyle-commerce identity, subscription emphasis, and uniform minimalism that could flatten the fan-specific archive.

### 8. NOWNESS

- URL: `https://www.nowness.com/`
- Adopted: art-direction-led cultural storytelling, expressive crop, and concise captioning.
- Rejected: video-platform behavior, autoplay, immersive takeover, and broad global-culture catalog.

### 9. Criterion site redesign note

- URL: `https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/5617-welcome-to-our-new-site`
- Adopted: explicit separation between article and film/catalog contexts, and layouts designed to give writers and visuals magazine-scale room.
- Rejected: replicating a complete commercial publication shell.

## Adopted synthesis

- one dominant cover visual rather than a card grid;
- black, cream, silver, and one burnt-vermilion signature color;
- Korean-first masthead, issue number, date, folios, source lines, captions, and archive index;
- asymmetrical column grids and large pull quotes;
- present-day feature pages contrasted with cooler, quieter archive pages;
- a personal note treated as marginalia, not a social post or diary application;
- a 680ms crop-and-mask Cover Reveal that preserves masthead and issue context.

## Rejected synthesis

- fan community, comments, chat, likes, follower counts, rankings, public profiles;
- SNS cards, infinite news feed, breaking-news urgency, gossip headlines;
- streaming playback, ticketing, merchandise, subscriptions, and purchase buttons;
- dashboards, analytics, recommendation scores, AI labels, sparkles, purple gradients, and glass cards;
- real artists, real teams, copyrighted promotional imagery, and copied magazine screens.

## Identity without real celebrity assets

The fan identity is created through repetition and editorial continuity rather than likeness:

- one fictional name and consistent fictional work titles;
- a reusable abstract profile built from geometric silhouette, silver paper, and vermilion light;
- recurring chronology marks, captions, issue folios, and archive labels;
- object-based stage and collection still lifes;
- first-person editorial notes that describe taste without exposing a real person's private records.
