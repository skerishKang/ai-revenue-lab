# Reference Notes

## Product boundary

This reference preserves official procedure, citizen experience, staff experience, field evidence, editorial inference and improvement follow-up as separate authorities. It is not a complaint board, case-management tool, survey wall, performance dashboard or government portal.

## Comparable public-service and procedure knowledge references

1. **GOV.UK Service Manual — Creating an experience map**
   Primary source: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/creating-an-experience-map
   Adopted: stage spine, event cards, visible pain points, evidence gathered over time. Rejected: sticky-note workshop aesthetics as the final product surface.
2. **GOV.UK Service Manual — Map and understand a user's whole problem**
   Primary source: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/map-a-users-whole-problem
   Adopted: user journey and service landscape shown together; online/offline touchpoints and evidence requirements. Rejected: any implication that this static reference operates a real service community.
3. **GOV.UK — Researching user experiences**
   Primary source: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/researching-user-experiences
   Adopted: numbered event records and separation of what happened, thought and felt. Rejected: real participant recording or personal data collection.
4. **OECD — Serving citizens: Measuring the performance of services for a better user experience**
   Primary source: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/serving-citizens_65223af7-en.html
   Adopted: combine subjective experience with objective service evidence, while avoiding unsupported aggregate claims. Rejected: decorative KPI dashboard.
5. **OECD — Digital Government Review of Korea, human-centred public services**
   Primary source: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/digital-government-review-of-korea_9defc197-en/full-report/delivering-human-centred-and-proactive-public-services_41783522.html
   Adopted: citizen and frontline staff perspectives, continuous improvement and follow-up. Rejected: Korean government branding or claims about actual Korean performance.

## Editorial, archival and information-design references

1. **U.S. National Archives Catalog Data Model** — hierarchy and relationship clarity, not its branding.
   https://www.archives.gov/research/data-model
2. **The National Archives Design System — Tables and metadata** — explicit captions, focusable overflow regions and restrained metadata treatment.
   https://design-system.nationalarchives.gov.uk/styles/tables/
3. **National Archives metadata requirements** — manifest thinking and interpretation context.
   https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/policy/metadata-compiled
4. **Information is Beautiful Awards — IEPS DATA** — accessible public-policy evidence storytelling. Adopted only the principle of transparent, curated evidence; no layout or proprietary artwork copied.
   https://www.informationisbeautifulawards.com/showcase/4902-ieps-data

## Visual decisions

- Paper atlas, field slips, envelopes, evidence receipts and annotations instead of rounded dashboard cards.
- Korean-first long-form copy with monospace authority labels.
- Official, citizen, staff and evidence layers remain readable without relying on colour alone.
- Mobile is a dedicated briefing, not a desktop shrink-down.

## Rejected patterns

Generic analytics dashboards, complaint feeds, government-portal chrome, CRM grids, legal case folders, real forms, surveillance visuals, stock office photography, proprietary branding and external runtime assets.
