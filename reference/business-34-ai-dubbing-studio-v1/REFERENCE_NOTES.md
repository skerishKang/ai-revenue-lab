# Reference Notes

## Comparable products and official sources

1. **Netflix Dubbing Creative Guidelines** — adopt original-intent preservation, performance direction and human dub-director authority; reject Netflix brand/UI and production delivery workflow.
2. **Adobe Premiere Text-Based Editing** — adopt speaker-labelled transcript and timecode association; reject video-editor timeline and live media editing.
3. **YouTube multi-language audio** — adopt explicit original/target language track identity; reject publishing, analytics and channel controls.
4. **Descript Translate and Dub Speech** — adopt review-before-lip-sync ordering and visible timing mismatch; reject one-click generation and provider UI.
5. **W3C media accessibility guidance** — adopt synchronized transcript/caption legibility and information equivalence; reject player implementation.

## Editorial and information-design references

- **BBC subtitle guidelines**: restrained line length and readable timing notation; no BBC identity or layout copied.
- **IBM Carbon data tables/status**: textual status labels and dense operational hierarchy; no Carbon component copy.
- **NASA mission-control documentation grammar**: hard rules, timecode rails and exception visibility; no insignia or screen copy.

## Adopted
- source-rights and speaker provenance before transformation;
- transcript correction separate from machine transcript;
- target timing budget beside translation choice;
- synthetic-voice identity separate from fictional source speaker;
- pronunciation and drift as durable review annotations;
- release authority only after human seal.

## Rejected
- generic video editor timeline;
- subtitle spreadsheet as the product identity;
- TTS playground, voice marketplace or celebrity clone;
- auto-approved master after generation;
- playback, upload, live waveform or actual media.

No third-party product branding, screenshots, proprietary assets, dialogue or layout was copied.
