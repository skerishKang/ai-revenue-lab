# Reference Notes

## Public-service and safety references

1. UN Women Safe Cities and Safe Public Spaces compendia — adopted locally relevant evidence, participatory constraints and the separation of lived safety from simplistic scoring; rejected claims that a route can guarantee safety.
2. U.S. DOT pedestrian safety material — adopted crossing, visibility, access and transport context as bounded environmental evidence; rejected live navigation and compliance claims.
3. GOV.UK service-design guidance — adopted explicit user needs, constraints and uncertainty; rejected government-portal chrome.
4. OpenStreetMap documentation — inspected map-layer conventions only; no map tiles, data or branding are used.

## Editorial references

- National Archives design system: adopted record hierarchy and status labels.
- Transit-map and field-notebook editorial grammar: adopted route strips, folios and annotation margins without copying a specific map.
- Readymag editorial examples: adopted asymmetry and proof-sheet composition, rejected interaction spectacle.

## Final decisions

The reference uses a fictional field board rather than a map application. Evidence, inference, uncertainty, accessibility and fallback remain separately labelled. No proprietary screen, logo, map geometry, copy or asset was copied.
