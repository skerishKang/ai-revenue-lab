# REFERENCE_NOTES.md

## Product boundary

Civic AI Navigator is a citizen-facing route explainer. It is not a government portal, chatbot, legal-advice interface, ticket system or submission service.

## Comparable civic-navigation products

1. **GOV.UK services and step-by-step navigation**
   Official sources: https://www.gov.uk/browse and https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/step-by-step-navigation/
   Adopt: end-to-end route clarity, explicit sequence, plain headings.
   Reject: copying GOV.UK branding, component code, forms or live service links.
2. **USAGov life events and Benefit Finder**
   Official sources: https://www.usa.gov/life-events and https://www.usa.gov/benefit-finder
   Adopt: begin from a citizen situation and group possible services by life event.
   Reject: eligibility determination, personalized results and actual program data.
3. **LifeSG**
   Official source: https://www.life.gov.sg/about-us
   Adopt: connect services around life moments rather than agency structure.
   Reject: account, personalization, reminders, applications and real cross-agency integration.
4. **NYC311**
   Official source: https://portal.311.nyc.gov/about-nyc-311/
   Adopt: a recognizable front door and clear human-contact boundary.
   Reject: complaints, requests, ticket status, phone channels and service submission.
5. **Ontario Design System**
   Official source: https://designsystem.ontario.ca/docs/documentation/explore/about-the-ods.html
   Adopt: accessible, consistent public-service hierarchy.
   Reject: Ontario branding and direct component replication.

## Editorial, wayfinding and information-design references

1. **GOV.UK Design System — layout and navigation**
   Sources: https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/layout/ and https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/navigate-a-service/
   Adopt: small-screen-first hierarchy, restrained width, visible route position.
2. **U.S. Web Design System**
   Sources: https://designsystem.digital.gov/ and https://designsystem.digital.gov/design-principles/
   Adopt: accessibility as a design constraint, clear identity and meaningful labels.
3. **Legible London / Transport for London wayfinding**
   Sources: https://tfl.gov.uk/modes/walking/find-your-way-around and https://tfl.gov.uk/corporate/website-accessibility/digital-accessibility-standard
   Adopt: route-first visual grammar, persistent landmarks, semantic structure.
   Reject: TfL colors, maps, symbols and transport branding.
4. **Ontario in-page navigation**
   Source: https://designsystem.ontario.ca/components/detail/in-page-navigation.html
   Adopt: scan-friendly section index and accessible focus order.

## Adopted visual decisions

- Civic atlas rather than search-result or portal framing.
- Hard rules, map lines, index slips, envelopes and signposts rather than rounded cards.
- Text trust labels in addition to color.
- Administrative phrase paired with citizen-friendly explanation.
- Human confirmation stays visible and is never presented as a failure state.
- Mobile is a dedicated action briefing rather than a reduced desktop atlas.

## Rejected patterns

- Generic chat bubbles and AI assistant persona.
- Real agency identity or form imitation.
- Eligibility or legal outcomes stated as certainty.
- Ticket status, CRM, analytics or omnichannel navigation.
- External runtime assets and copied proprietary layouts.

No referenced product brand, complete page, copy, map or asset is reproduced.
